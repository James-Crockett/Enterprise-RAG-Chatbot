# Performance review process

Last reviewed: 2026-01-28 by Aisha Karim, Head of People

## Review cycle

Kestrel runs performance reviews twice a year, aligned to the calendar-year fiscal year:

- Mid-year check-in: completed by July 15
- Annual review: completed by January 15, covering the full prior calendar year

Both cycles use the same form in the HR portal. The mid-year check-in is lighter weight and does not affect compensation directly, while the annual review ties into merit increases and bonus decisions.

## Steps in the annual cycle

1. Self-assessment: employees complete a self-assessment in the HR portal by December 1, covering goals set at the start of the year and notable contributions.
2. Peer feedback: employees select 2 to 4 peers, and managers can add up to 2 more. Peer feedback is due by December 10.
3. Manager review: managers write the formal review, drawing on the self-assessment and peer feedback, due by January 5.
4. Calibration: managers within each department meet with Marcus Lee (engineering) or the relevant VP to calibrate ratings across the team, so similar performance lands on similar ratings regardless of manager.
5. Delivery: managers deliver the final review in a 1:1 conversation by January 15, followed by written access to the review in the HR portal.

## Rating scale

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Exceeds expectations | Consistently above the bar for the role, often taking on scope beyond it |
| Meets expectations | Solid, reliable performance at the expected level |
| Partially meets expectations | Some gaps, with a clear improvement plan attached |
| Does not meet expectations | Significant gaps, managed through a formal improvement plan with HR involved |

## Compensation timing

Merit increases and bonus payouts tied to the annual review take effect in the first pay period of March, reflected in the semi-monthly pay cycle. This gives payroll and finance time to process calibrated ratings after the January 15 delivery deadline.

## Improvement plans

An employee rated "does not meet expectations," or with a specific serious concern flagged mid-cycle, goes on a formal improvement plan. Aisha Karim's team drafts the plan with the manager, typically running 30, 60, or 90 days depending on the issue. Progress is reviewed at each milestone, and the plan sets out clearly what happens if goals aren't met by the end date.

## New hires and partial years

Employees who started fewer than 90 days before a review deadline are excluded from that cycle's formal review and instead get an informal 90-day check-in with their manager. They participate normally starting with the next full cycle.

## Disputing a review

If an employee disagrees with their rating, they can request a review meeting with their manager and Aisha Karim's team within 10 business days of delivery. This does not guarantee a rating change, but it does guarantee the concern is documented and considered.

## FAQ

**Do contractors go through this process?** No, contractor performance is managed through the contract terms with the hiring manager, not the HR portal review cycle.

**Can I see my peer feedback verbatim?** No, peer feedback is summarized and anonymized by the manager before it reaches the review conversation.
