# Procurement policy

Last reviewed: 2026-02-12 by Elena Vasquez, CFO

## When procurement applies

Any purchase, or set of related purchases, totaling more than $5,000 goes through Coupa and a procurement review, regardless of department. This includes software licenses, hardware orders, consulting engagements, and marketing spend. Purchases under $5,000 follow the standard expense or software request process instead.

## Procurement steps

1. Create a purchase request in Coupa describing the item or service, the vendor, the cost, and the business justification.
2. Your manager approves the request first, confirming it's within the team's budget.
3. Finance reviews the request for budget availability and, for anything recurring, checks against existing vendor spend to avoid duplication.
4. For a new vendor, Ruth Adeyemi's team reviews the contract terms, and Tom Brennan's team reviews security if the vendor touches customer data or connects to internal systems.
5. Once all reviews clear, Coupa issues a purchase order, and the vendor can be paid against it.

## Timeline

A straightforward request with an existing, already-vetted vendor typically clears in 5 to 7 business days. A new vendor, especially one requiring a security review or contract negotiation, can take 3 to 4 weeks. Plan large purchases well ahead of when the item or service is actually needed.

## New vendor onboarding

New vendors need to go through a one-time onboarding step in Coupa before any purchase order is issued, covering:

- Basic vendor information and banking details for payment
- A signed contract or order form, reviewed by Ruth Adeyemi's team
- A security questionnaire for any vendor with system access or data handling, reviewed by Tom Brennan's team

Once onboarded, a vendor doesn't need to repeat this step for future purchases, unless the contract terms change materially.

## Budget ownership

Each department has an annual budget managed by its VP: Priya Raman for engineering infrastructure and tooling, Jordan Pike for sales tools and travel, Nina Castellanos for product research, and so on. Procurement requests are checked against the requesting department's remaining budget for the year, tracked in Coupa alongside the finance system.

## Emergency purchases

An urgent purchase needed before the standard review can complete (for example, replacing failed production infrastructure) can be flagged "emergency" in Coupa. This routes for same-day review from Elena Vasquez or a delegate, but still requires retroactive completion of the standard review steps within 5 business days.

## Recurring contracts and renewals

Coupa tracks contract renewal dates and flags them to the budget owner 60 days ahead of renewal. Renewals over $5,000 go through the same review process as a new purchase, though an existing, already-reviewed vendor typically moves faster on renewal than on first onboarding.

## Splitting purchases to avoid review

Breaking a purchase into smaller pieces to stay under the $5,000 threshold is not permitted and is treated as a policy violation if identified, regardless of intent. Finance periodically audits Expensify submissions for patterns like this.

## FAQ

**Can I start using a vendor's service before the purchase order is issued?** No, work with a new vendor should not begin until the purchase order is issued, since there's no contractual protection or payment guarantee before that point.

**Who approves procurement requests when a VP is out of office?** The VP's designated delegate, set in Coupa, approves in their place; check with your manager if you're unsure who that is for your department.
